4.3.1 Measurement validation¶

We validate the three latent constructs used—

  • Endurance/Institutionalisation (EEI)
  • Adaptive Capacity
  • Legitimacy/Efficacy (LEF)

Strategy

  1. Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) to discover structure
  2. Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) to test fit and reliability.

This sequence reflects best practice for scale development and measurement models (see Fabrigar & Wegener, 2011; Brown, 2015).

Indicators (what loads on each factor)

EEI: Breadth_Spatial, Breadth_Subject, Strategy_Diversity, Objective_Diversity, Relations, Coord_Vertical, Coord_Horizontal (and—when available—Authority_Treaty, Authority_BindingSecondary, Authority_Delegated).

Adaptive_Capacity: mean of Strategy_Diversity and Objective_Diversity (0–10).

LEF: Relations, Coord_Vertical, Coord_Horizontal, emphasis sub indices from Objectives/Strategies (e.g., inclusion & rights; monitoring & accountability; policy & regulation), and—when available—authority components.

We run EFA on the across-IGO scaled indicators (z-standardised) using principal-axis/ML extraction and oblique rotation (oblimin/geomin) because we expect correlated dimensions.

  • We first verify data adequacy with the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin Value (KMO) (≥ 0.60 acceptable) and Bartlett’s test (p < 0.001).
  • The number of factors is chosen by Horn’s parallel analysis (primary rule), with scree and interpretability as secondary checks.
  • Items must load ≥ 0.40 on their factor with cross loading separation ≥ 0.30; problematic items are reviewed for conceptual fit rather than retained mechanically.
  • Factor scores are saved by regression for robustness checks following Horn (1965), and Kaiser (1974).

We then specify single-factor CFA models for EEI, Adaptive Capacity, and LEF in lavaan (R), using MLR (robust) estimation with Full Information Maximum Likelihood (FIML) for any incidental missingness.

  • Model fit is judged against widely used criteria—RMSEA ≤ .06 (good / .08 acceptable), CFI/TLI ≥ .95/.90, SRMR ≤ .08—noting that indices capture different misspecifications and should be read jointly rather than by a single hard cutoff.
  • Modification indices are inspected sparingly and only when theoretically defensible.
  • Reliability is reported as composite reliability/omega and AVE (convergent validity).
  • Standardised factor scores (mean 0, SD 1) feed the regressions in section 4.3.2–4.3.4 below, and are consistent with Goretzko et al (2023); Hu & Bentler (2009;) and Rosseel (2012).
  • EFA is implemented with psych (R); CFA with lavaan. We archive scripts and session information along with the data objects described in Section 4.2.5 above (Data objects and transparency)

The exploratory factor analysis produced a six‑factor solution, indicating that the indicators do not form a single coherent EEI dimension but instead separate into distinct thematic clusters. Spatial_jurisdiction and mandate_breadth loaded very strongly on the first factor, forming a clear institutional breadth/scope dimension, while subject_matter_jurisdiction showed only a moderate and cross‑loading pattern. Strategy‑related and objective‑related indicators split into two separate factors, reflecting distinct strategic and goal‑setting capacities rather than a unified construct. Coordination and relational variables loaded on two additional factors—one capturing vertical/relational legitimacy and another representing horizontal coordination—demonstrating that these items belong to a different conceptual domain (LEF) rather than EEI. Authority indicators also separated into their own factor, further confirming that legal authority is empirically distinct from breadth and coordination. Overall, the EFA shows that the EEI indicator set is multidimensional, with only the breadth variables forming a strong, interpretable factor. This supports a more parsimonious EEI measurement model focused on institutional scope rather than a broad, multi‑component construct.

The confirmatory factor analysis shows that only spatial_jurisdiction loads strongly and meaningfully on the EEI latent factor, with a standardized loading of 0.60, indicating a moderate and statistically stable association. All other indicators perform poorly. Subject_matter_jurisdiction (Std. = 0.08, p = 0.72) and mandate_breadth (Std. = 0.55, p = 0.67) exhibit weak, statistically non‑significant loadings, suggesting they do not reliably reflect the underlying EEI construct in this specification. The authority indicators also fail to load: authority_BindingSecondary_Factor (Std. = –0.07, p = 0.77) and authority_delegated_factor (Std. = 0.07, p = 0.76) show negligible and non‑significant relationships with EEI. Overall, the model indicates that the EEI factor is not well‑identified with the current indicator set, and only spatial_jurisdiction provides meaningful measurement information. This pattern is consistent with the EFA results, which showed a clean breadth factor dominated by spatial_jurisdiction and mandate_breadth, but with weaker contributions from other items. A more parsimonious EEI model—focused on the strongest breadth indicators—would likely yield better fit and construct validity.

def compute_omega(loadings):
    """
    loadings: array-like of standardized factor loadings
    """
    loadings = np.array(loadings)
    # Error variances = 1 - loading^2
    errors = 1 - loadings**2
    omega = (loadings.sum()**2) / ((loadings.sum()**2) + errors.sum())
    return omega

def compute_ave(loadings):
    loadings = np.array(loadings)
    ave = np.mean(loadings**2)
    return ave
