## 4.3.1 Measurement validation

This section evaluates whether the modelling indicators used to operationalise Endurance and Institutionalisation (EEI), Adaptive Capacity, and Legitimacy and Efficacy (LEF) behave as coherent measurement sets prior to conjecture testing. The goal is measurement validation rather than substantive inference, in line with guidance that construct operationalisation should be assessed for empirical coherence and separation before it is used in causal models (Adcock & Collier, 2001). To do so, the chapter follows a two-step strategy that is standard in scale development and measurement modelling, beginning with exploratory factor analysis (EFA) to map empirical structure and followed by confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to test single-factor specifications and report fit and reliability (Fabrigar & Wegener, 2011; Brown, 2015).

### Data and preprocessing

All analyses in this section are generated deterministically from the FULL-normalised modelling table (`data/analysis_dataset_full.csv`), which is assembled from the matrix-wide IGO × category scores for the attribute families described in §4.2 and the derived indices defined in §4.2.4. In the R workflow (`scripts/07_measurement_validation.R`), `readr::read_csv()` ingests the analysis table, and `dplyr` is used to (i) align variable names to the construct notation used in this chapter and (ii) form the indicator pools for EEI, Adaptive Capacity, and LEF. Indicators are z-standardised across IGOs using `scale()` to ensure comparability across mixed 0–10 indices and continuous covariates; this follows common practice when indicators are on different bounded scales and the analytical focus is on covariance structure rather than original units (Brown, 2015).

The indicator pools are defined as follows. EEI is evaluated using a breadth-authority-capacity pool that includes scope, strategy and objective diversity, coordination and relational indicators, and authority components when available. Adaptive Capacity is evaluated using the strategy and objective indicators that constitute the index construction in §4.2.4. LEF is evaluated using relational and coordination indicators together with intersection and authority components, reflecting the measurement logic that legitimacy and efficacy are expressed through observable relational positioning and governance-function emphasis (Rosseel, 2012; Brown, 2015).

### Factorability checks and factor retention

Before estimating factor models, the indicator pools are tested for factorability using the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) measure and Bartlett’s test of sphericity. The KMO statistic, implemented as `psych::KMO()`, assesses whether partial correlations are sufficiently small relative to bivariate correlations for common-factor modelling to be meaningful (Kaiser, 1974). Bartlett’s test, computed using `psych::cortest.bartlett()`, evaluates whether the correlation matrix differs from an identity matrix, which is a basic admissibility condition for factor analysis (Bartlett, 1950). Results are reported in Table 4.E1, including the overall KMO and per-variable MSA diagnostics.

The number of factors retained for the exploratory solutions is chosen by Horn’s parallel analysis as the primary decision rule, with scree and interpretability used as secondary checks (Horn, 1965; Fabrigar & Wegener, 2011). Parallel analysis is implemented in `psych::fa.parallel()` and archived as Figure 4.E1, which plots observed eigenvalues against eigenvalues from simulated random data with the same N and p. This rule guards against over-extraction that can occur under simple Kaiser greater-than-one heuristics, particularly in modest samples (Horn, 1965; Brown, 2015).

### Exploratory factor analysis

EFA is used to diagnose whether the indicator pools separate into coherent dimensions that resemble the conceptual families specified in §4.2. In the R pipeline, EFA is estimated using `psych::fa()` with principal-axis extraction (`fm = "pa"`) and oblique rotation (`rotate = "oblimin"`). Oblique rotation is preferred because design, authority, strategy, and coordination attributes are expected to covary in institutional fields rather than form orthogonal dimensions (Fabrigar & Wegener, 2011). The rotated pattern matrices are reported in Table 4.E2, with a compact factor-interpretation summary in Table 4.E3 that lists indicators with absolute loadings at or above 0.40 and supports post hoc factor labelling. A loading threshold of 0.40 is a commonly used reporting rule in applied factor analysis, but the interpretation is guided by construct meaning rather than applied mechanically (Brown, 2015).

For EEI, the EFA provides an empirical map of how scope, authority, and organisational-capacity indicators cluster, allowing the chapter to assess whether a single coherent dimension is plausible or whether the indicator set is multi-dimensional. For LEF, the EFA serves a similar purpose, checking whether relational and coordination indicators form a coherent latent dimension or separate into vertical-relational versus horizontal coordination subdimensions. These exploratory results motivate the parsimonious confirmatory specifications that follow, and they provide a transparent basis for excluding indicators that appear empirically distinct from the intended construct domain (Fabrigar & Wegener, 2011; Adcock & Collier, 2001).

### Confirmatory factor analysis and fit evaluation

CFA is used to evaluate single-factor specifications for EEI, Adaptive Capacity, and LEF, primarily to test whether each construct can be operationalised as a reflective latent dimension with acceptable fit and interpretable loadings. Models are estimated in `lavaan` using `lavaan::cfa()` with the robust MLR estimator and Full Information Maximum Likelihood (FIML) for any incidental missingness, following standard practice for SEM under heteroskedasticity and incomplete data (Rosseel, 2012; Brown, 2015). Fit statistics are reported in Table 4.E4, and standardised loadings with uncertainty are reported in Table 4.E5.

Fit is assessed using RMSEA, CFI, TLI, and SRMR, with the caveat that widely used cut-offs should be treated as descriptive guidelines rather than rigid pass-fail rules because they vary in sensitivity to model size, misspecification type, and sample size (Hu & Bentler, 1999; Brown, 2015). Modification indices are inspected only as a diagnostic and are acted upon only when the implied changes are theoretically defensible. For Adaptive Capacity, the two-indicator single-factor model is identified by applying explicit constraints, and it is therefore just-identified with degrees of freedom equal to zero; in this case, global fit indices are not informative and the model is treated primarily as a structured way to generate factor scores consistent with the measurement logic in §4.2.4 (Brown, 2015).

### Reliability and convergent validity

Reliability and convergent validity are reported for the CFA models using composite reliability and Average Variance Extracted (AVE). AVE is reported following the standard definition proposed by Fornell and Larcker, which summarises the proportion of variance in indicators captured by the latent construct (Fornell & Larcker, 1981). Reliability is reported using composite reliability and omega-style measures computed from the fitted model, which is consistent with recommendations to assess internal consistency using model-based quantities rather than only alpha-style heuristics (Raykov, 1997; Brown, 2015). These outputs are generated with `semTools` and archived in Table 4.E6 (Jorgensen et al., 2021; Rosseel, 2012).

### Factor scores, outputs, and reproducibility

For downstream modelling in §4.3.2–§4.3.4, standardised factor scores are generated from each CFA model using regression scoring (`lavaan::lavPredict()`), and then re-standardised to mean zero and standard deviation one for interpretability across outcomes and specifications (Brown, 2015). The consolidated scores table is exported as `outputs/tables/measurement_validation_scores.csv` and is used as an input to the conjecture-specific regression workflows.

To support research integrity, all tables and figures in this section are produced by a single scripted pipeline (`scripts/07_measurement_validation.R`). Outputs are written as thesis-ready Excel workbooks using `openxlsx`, and the script writes a run log with parameter settings and `sessionInfo()` to `outputs/logs/07_measurement_validation_log.txt`. This aligns with reproducible research guidance that emphasises scripted provenance, explicit parameterisation, and archivable computational environments (Peng, 2011; Stodden, Seiler, & Ma, 2018).
